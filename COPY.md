# Crackbotics — taglines and site copy

Working notes for the words on the site and flyer. Nothing here is load-bearing;
swap in whatever sounds like you.

## Taglines

The one currently used on the site and flyer:

> **Stop overpaying for aluminum.**

Short enough for a Discord status, a shirt, or the top of a flyer. Alternates,
roughly in order of how much I'd actually use them:

| Tagline | Angle |
|---|---|
| CNC aluminum without the CNC price. | Price, plainly stated |
| Your parts. This week. Half the price. | Price + speed in one breath |
| Made by FTC students, for FTC teams. | Credibility / community |
| Skip the shipping wait. | Speed only — good as a sub-line |
| Cheap aluminum, fast. No catch. | Blunt, scrappy |
| The parts you wish you could afford. | Softer, more relatable |

One-line descriptors (for a Discord server description, Instagram bio, or the
`<meta description>`):

- Custom CNC aluminum for FTC teams — cheaper than the big suppliers, 1–3 day
  local delivery or shop pickup in San Diego.
- Plates, beams, standoffs and brackets on an 8 mm grid, machined by students
  from Runtime Terror (#22105) and All Systems Go (#21239).

## Voice

A few rules that kept the copy consistent — worth keeping if you rewrite:

1. **Say the number, not the adjective.** "1–3 days" beats "fast turnaround."
2. **Name the competition.** Undercutting SendCutSend and goBILDA is the whole
   pitch; being coy about it wastes the strongest line you have.
3. **Sound like a team, not a vendor.** "We've waited on the same overpriced
   parts you have" does more work than any feature list.
4. **No superlatives you can't back.** "Way cheaper" is fine. "The best CNC
   shop in California" invites someone to check.
5. **Short sentences.** People read a bulletin board at walking speed.

## Claims worth being careful with

You're making direct price comparisons against two named companies, which is
legal and normal — but keep it defensible:

- **"We heavily undercut SendCutSend and goBILDA"** is a claim about *your*
  prices. Keep it truthful and current. If you later post a comparison table
  with their actual prices, date it ("prices checked Sept 2026") so a stale
  screenshot doesn't become a false claim.
- **"goBILDA-style standoffs"** describes compatibility, not origin. Don't call
  them goBILDA standoffs, don't use goBILDA's logo or product photos, and don't
  copy their patented hole *pattern* — the 8 mm grid spacing itself is fine,
  which is why the copy says "8 mm grid spacing" rather than anything more
  specific.
- **Team names.** Runtime Terror and All Systems Go are your teams, but the
  teams themselves (and their sponsors) may have opinions about being named on
  a commercial flyer. Worth a quick heads-up to your coaches — cheaper than
  finding out at a competition.
- **The <i>FIRST</i>® trademark.** You can say you serve FTC teams and that you
  compete. You can't imply FIRST endorses you. The disclaimer in the footer of
  both the site and the flyer covers this; keep it there.
- **Non-affiliation line.** Present on both pieces. Don't delete it to save
  space on the flyer — it's the cheapest insurance on the page.

## Things to fill in before launch

- A real Discord invite (non-expiring, or it dies on the bulletin board).
- A real email address on your own domain if you can — `hello@crackbotics.com`
  reads better than a personal Gmail on a flyer.
- Actual prices. Right now the site says "ask us." That's fine to start, but a
  few anchor prices ("standoffs from $X", "plates from $Y") convert far better
  than a quote form, because the whole pitch is that you're cheap. As soon as
  you know your numbers, put three of them on the site.
- Photos. Even one good shot of a finished part beats three placeholders.
